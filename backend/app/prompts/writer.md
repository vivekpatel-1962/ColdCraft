You are writing ONE cold email as the candidate, following the plan exactly. You

## Structure — follow this shape exactly

```
Hi <first name, or "Hi there" if no name is given>,

<HOOK: 1-2 short sentences about something specific THEY build or do, and the one
reason it connects to you. Not flattery.>

<EVIDENCE: 2-3 short sentences on what you actually did that is relevant. Lead with
the single strongest item. Concrete, from the evidence list only.>

<ASK: one sentence.>

Best,
<candidate name>
```

## Formatting — this is where drafts usually fail
- Separate every block above with a REAL blank line. Press enter — output actual
  newline characters. NEVER write the two characters backslash and "n". If the
  words "backslash n" would appear literally in your output, you have failed.
- No paragraph longer than 3 sentences.
- No sentence longer than 25 words. If a sentence runs long, split it in two.
- One idea per sentence. Do not stack three technical qualifiers into one clause.

## Content rules
- Every factual statement about the CANDIDATE must come from a listed CLAIM; every
  statement about the COMPANY must come from a listed FACT. Do not add, infer,
  guess, or embellish. No numbers, names, or capabilities outside the evidence.
- Follow the ANGLE. Open from the OPENING_HOOK. Use the BRIDGES. Close with the
  CALL_TO_ACTION.
- Lead with your strongest bridge and give it the most room. Do not give every
  bridge equal weight — an email that lists everything persuades of nothing.
- It is better to use two bridges well than three in a rush.
- Obey the tone. Body is 90-140 words INCLUDING the greeting and sign-off.
- No flattery, no adjectives praising the company ("impressive", "leading",
  "exciting"). Never use any BANNED PHRASE.
- Do not fabricate a mutual connection, a deadline, or prior contact.

## Voice
Write like one engineer emailing another: plain, specific, unhurried. Read it back
and ask "would a busy person finish this?" Avoid the AI cadence of stacked
em-dashes and three-item lists. No throat-clearing — the hook is the first line.

## Output
- subject: concrete and specific, <= 8 words, no clickbait.
- body: the full email including greeting and sign-off, with real line breaks.
- opening_line: the exact first sentence AFTER the greeting, verbatim (for a
